# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Official acts:
- Its members voted unanimously against a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Its members voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Four of its ten members voted for a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies, and six voted against it.
Influence:
- Endorsed a bill to make the expanded premium subsidies permanent and to reverse the health coverage reductions in the budget law.
- Issued a statement opposing the budget law for cutting public health coverage for low-income residents and for adding to public debt.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- Introduced, as lead sponsor, a bill to enroll every resident in a single public health plan covering medical, dental, vision, hearing, mental health, and long-term care, with no premiums, deductibles, or copayments.
- Voted against a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted against a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
Influence:
- Co-led a public hearing to press colleagues to join the single public health plan bill, arguing it is the only option that guarantees care for everyone.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Record B's official acts include lead sponsorship of a universal single-payer health plan and consistent votes protecting coverage and food assistance, whereas Record A's members split on a funding bill that did not extend the premium subsidies.

</details>

## Pair 2

**Record A**

Official acts:
- Voted against a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted for a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted for an earlier temporary funding bill that did not extend the premium subsidies.
Statements:
- When casting that earlier vote, pledged to keep working on a cross-party extension of the expanded premium subsidies through the following year.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- In government, enacted above-inflation increases for four years in the standard rate of the main benefit for working-age people.
- In government, cut the health-related supplement to that benefit by about half for new applicants and stopped raising it with inflation, while leaving current recipients and people with the most severe lifelong conditions unaffected.
- In government, proposed tightening eligibility for the main disability benefit, then withdrew the change after opposition and began a review of how that benefit is assessed.
- In government, enacted a law removing the rule that limited child-related benefit payments to a family's first two children.
- In government, restored an annual heating payment to about three quarters of retirees, after limiting it the previous winter to retirees receiving low-income benefits.
- In government, set day-to-day funding for the public health service to grow by an average of 3 percent a year above inflation in its multi-year spending plan.
- In government, extended free school meals to all children in households receiving the main benefit for working-age people.
Statements:
- The party leader, as head of government, announced the intention to create a public care service offering free personal care for older people in the next legislative term, funded in part by making the rule for annual public pension increases less generous from 2030.
- The head of government pledged to reduce spending on working-age benefits while ruling out abrupt cuts.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Record B shows enacted expansions across income support and health, including above-inflation benefit increases, abolition of the two-child limit, restored heating payments, real-terms health service funding growth and expanded free school meals, which outweigh its cuts to the health supplement and the withdrawn disability tightening; Record A consists mostly of votes for or against other actors' bills, and several of those votes went for funding bills that let the subsidies lapse.

</details>

## Pair 4

**Record A**

Official acts:
- In government, extended a public dental plan to all uninsured adults aged 18 to 64 with household income below a set threshold.
- In government, made a national school meals program permanent.
- In government, passed a budget that continued the public dental plan, public drug coverage, and subsidized child care but added no new funding for them.
- In government, provided no new funding for further public drug coverage agreements with regional governments beyond the four already signed.
Statements:
- Said in its budget that it was protecting social programs, naming child care, dental care, and drug coverage.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- Its members voted unanimously against a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Its members voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Its members who voted all voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
Influence:
- Published a health plan urging lawmakers to keep the expanded premium subsidies, reverse recent reductions in public health coverage for low-income residents, simplify enrollment, and extend a cap on out-of-pocket prescription costs to people with private insurance. The plan does not propose a single public health plan or a public insurance option.
- Co-led a letter, joined by 208 legislators, telling the chamber's leader to drop any legislation that would cut the public pension or public health coverage for older or low-income residents.
- Its chair publicly demanded an immediate vote on a three-year extension of the expanded premium subsidies.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Record B's official acts consistently defend and extend coverage and income support: votes against cutting public health coverage and food assistance and for extending premium subsidies. Record A, by contrast, pairs one dental coverage expansion with budgets that froze funding and halted further public drug coverage agreements.

</details>

## Pair 5

**Record A**

Official acts:
- Its members voted unanimously for the final version of a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Four of its members voted in committee against advancing an earlier version of that bill while pressing for its work requirements and spending reductions to take effect sooner, then abstained to let it advance.
- Its chair and several of its members cosponsored a bill to phase down over eight years the higher national funding share for adults added to low-income public health coverage until it equals the regular share.
- Its chair voted against a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance, and no member of the group is recorded among the legislators from its party who voted for it.
Influence:
- Its chair warned party leaders that members would withhold their votes from any funding bill carrying an extension of the expanded premium subsidies, and several members publicly vowed to block one.
- Its governing board declared it would reject any attempt by the other chamber to weaken the spending reductions in the budget bill.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- Voted for a law that enrolls every resident in publicly funded health coverage with no charge at the point of care.
- Voted to raise the minimum public pension and to tie unemployment benefits to wage growth.
- Voted against a budget that reduced public health spending.
Influence:
- Publicly pressed fellow legislators to drop a proposed charge for doctor visits.
Statements:
- Announced the intention to introduce a bill adding dental and eye care to public coverage.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Record B's official acts directly expand guaranteed health coverage and income support, including universal publicly funded care, higher pensions, and wage-linked unemployment benefits, whereas Record A's official acts cut public health coverage and food assistance and oppose subsidy extensions.

## Pair 8

**Record A**

- Voted for a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
Statements:
- Said the vote for that funding bill was cast because a dispute over insurance premiums should not be fought by withholding pay from public workers and food assistance from low-income families.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- Nearly all of its members voted for a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- A large majority of its members, including its chair and vice chair, voted against a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Nearly all of its members voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
Influence:
- Published a framework urging its party to pass a bill that redirects premium subsidies from insurers into personal health accounts held by patients.
- In the same framework, urged making all non-citizens ineligible for public health coverage for low-income residents, food assistance, and housing assistance.
- In the same framework, urged ending a rule that grants automatic benefit eligibility to people enrolled in other aid programs, and adding asset tests.
- In the same framework, urged funding payments that lower out-of-pocket costs for lower-income people with individually purchased insurance, which it said would cut premiums by 11 percent and reduce public spending.
- In the same framework, urged requiring public health coverage for low-income residents to pay hospitals the same rate wherever care is delivered, which it counted as a large spending reduction.
- In the same framework, urged eliminating a dedicated fund for disease prevention and public health programs.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A voted against cutting public health coverage and food assistance and voted to extend premium subsidies, while Record B's members largely voted for those cuts and against the extension, and its framework mostly urged further benefit restrictions.

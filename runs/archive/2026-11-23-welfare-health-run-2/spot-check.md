# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Official acts:
- Works to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage, voting against reducing it and for extending its subsidies.
- Voted against a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Cosponsored a bill to enroll every resident in a single public health plan covering medical, dental, vision, hearing, mental health, and long-term care, with no premiums, deductibles, or copayments.
- Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted against a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
Influence:
- At a rally in a district held by the majority party, criticized the local legislator for voting for a budget plan that an official analysis found would require reductions in public health coverage for low-income residents.
Statements:
- Said in floor remarks that they could not support that funding bill because it left out an extension of the premium subsidies and took money from food assistance and health care.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- Works to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage, voting against reducing it and for extending its subsidies.
- Voted against a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted against a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
Influence:
- As a party leader in the chamber, publicly demanded that the chamber's leader bring a three-year extension of the expanded premium subsidies to a vote at once.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Both records share the same official acts protecting existing coverage and subsidies, but Record A also cosponsored a bill for universal single-payer coverage with no cost-sharing, an official act that goes beyond Record B's influence-tier demand for a subsidy vote.

</details>

## Pair 6

**Record A**

Official acts:
- In government, keeps in place a tax-funded health service that is available to every resident and free of charge for doctor and hospital care.
- In government, has kept the rule that raises the public pension every year by the highest of prices, wages, or 2.5 percent.
- In government, enacted above-inflation increases for four years in the standard rate of the main benefit for working-age people.
- In government, cut the health-related supplement to that benefit by about half for new applicants and stopped raising it with inflation, while leaving current recipients and people with the most severe lifelong conditions unaffected.
- In government, proposed tightening eligibility for the main disability benefit, then withdrew the change after opposition and began a review of how that benefit is assessed.
- In government, enacted a law removing the rule that limited child-related benefit payments to a family's first two children.
- In government, restored an annual heating payment to about three quarters of retirees, after limiting it the previous winter to retirees receiving low-income benefits.
- In government, set day-to-day funding for the public health service to grow by an average of 3 percent a year above inflation in its multi-year spending plan.
- In government, extended free school meals to all children in households receiving the main benefit for working-age people.
Statements:
- The party leader, as head of government, announced the intention to create a public care service offering free personal care for older people in the next legislative term, funded in part by making the rule for annual public pension increases less generous from 2030.
- The head of government pledged to reduce spending on working-age benefits while ruling out abrupt cuts.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- Works to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage, voting against reducing it and for extending its subsidies.
- Voted against a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted for a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted for an earlier temporary funding bill that did not extend the premium subsidies.
Statements:
- When casting that earlier vote, pledged to keep working on a cross-party extension of the expanded premium subsidies through the following year.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A keeps in place and funds a universal free health service and broad income supports such as pension uprating, benefit increases and removal of the two-child limit, which outweighs its partial cuts, while Record B mainly defends a non-universal system that leaves some residents uninsured and its subsidy votes are mixed.

</details>

## Pair 7

**Record A**

Official acts:
- Five of its eight legislators voted against a law removing the rule that limited child-related benefit payments to a family's first two children, and two voted for it.
- Its three voting legislators voted against a law that raised the standard rate of the main benefit for working-age people above inflation and cut its health-related supplement by about half for new applicants.
Influence:
- Announced a plan to abolish the main disability benefit and the health-related supplement to the main benefit for working-age adults, replacing them with a regularly reviewed allowance for the most severely ill and locally run support accounts, to cut benefit spending substantially.
Statements:
- Says the public health service will always stay free at the point of use, while its leader has repeatedly said the way that service is funded should be re-examined and has been open to other funding models.

**Record B**

Official acts:
- Voted to leave public health coverage for older residents intact: the budget law cut none of that coverage's benefits for citizens. In the existing system, public health coverage is guaranteed only to older, disabled, and low-income residents, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage.
- Voted for a budget law that reduced funding for public health coverage for low-income residents, added work requirements to that coverage, and reduced food assistance.
- In committee, voted against advancing an earlier version of that bill while pressing for its work requirements and spending reductions to take effect sooner, then abstained to let it advance once the work requirements were moved earlier.
- Introduced a bill, and filed an amendment to the budget bill, to phase down over eight years the higher national funding share for adults added to low-income public health coverage until it equals the regular share, and to let regional governments narrow eligibility for that group.
- Voted against a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance.
- Voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
- Voted for a funding bill that did not extend the expiring premium subsidies.
Influence:
- Led a letter, signed by 19 other legislators, urging colleagues to lower the national funding share for adults added to low-income public health coverage to the regular share, to limit the financing arrangements regional governments use to draw national funds, and to rule out further expansion of that coverage.
- Publicly urged party colleagues to let the expanded premium subsidies expire, and said they should not expect support for any deal that extends them.
Statements:
- After the committee vote, said the budget bill needed further changes before final passage, including an end to the financing arrangements that let regional governments draw additional national funds for public health coverage.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Both records oppose some benefit expansions, but Record B's official acts consistently cut low-income health coverage and food assistance, add work requirements, and block premium subsidy extensions, while Record A's acts are more mixed (it opposed a law that halved a health supplement) and it pledges to keep the health service free.

</details>

# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Its members vote to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage.
Income support it keeps in place:
- No recorded act.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously against a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Its members voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] None of its members voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
- [Influence] Issued a statement opposing the budget law for cutting public health coverage for low-income residents and for adding to public debt.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously against the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Endorsed a bill to make the expanded premium subsidies permanent and to reverse the health coverage reductions in the budget law.

**Record B**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Influence] Its alternative budget proposed no change to the public health insurance that covers everyone who works or lives legally in the country. The only health measure reported in it was replacing medical aid for undocumented migrants with emergency-only care.
Income support it keeps in place:
- No recorded act.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- No recorded act.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Its legislators voted unanimously for a suspension of a law raising the retirement age, which freezes that age until 2028.
- [Official act] Its legislators voted against the annual social insurance budget, which carried that suspension and dropped a planned freeze on pensions and minimum benefits.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Published an alternative budget proposing to end non-contributory benefits for foreigners who have not worked full-time for at least five years.
- [Influence] In the same alternative budget, proposed lifting a freeze on social benefits and returning the retirement age to 60 or 62.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A's official acts consistently defend and expand health coverage and food assistance by opposing cuts and extending subsidies, while Record B's support for universal coverage rests only on influence-tier proposals and its official acts on income support are mixed, including a vote against a budget that dropped a pension freeze.

</details>

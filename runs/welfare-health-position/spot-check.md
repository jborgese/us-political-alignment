# Welfare and health: position pilot: blinded spot check

Question: Which record's position, as its evidence shows it, is closer to government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- No recorded act.
Income support it keeps in place:
- No recorded act.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Statement] Before the floor vote on the budget bill, said it would be on course to pass once it added further reductions to national spending on public health coverage for working-age adults, which they said would curb waste and fraud.
- [Statement] Said the expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased insurance should end and subsidies return to their earlier, smaller level.
Changes to income support in the period:
- No recorded act.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Statement] Called for comprehensive national reform of health care and health insurance, saying the existing law on individually purchased insurance had failed to make coverage affordable and accessible.

**Record B**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- No recorded act.
Income support it keeps in place:
- No recorded act.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Statement] Vowed to keep fighting a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Statement] Vowed to keep fighting the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Statement] Planned to press, in the next legislative term, for committee action on a bill to enroll every resident in a single public health plan.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Record B opposes cuts to public health coverage and food assistance and pushes for a single public health plan covering every resident, while Record A supports cutting public health coverage and shrinking premium subsidies.

</details>

## Pair 2

**Record A**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Statement] Its new leader campaigned for the leadership on keeping the existing public insurance for doctor and hospital care and extending it to prescription drugs and to dental, vision, hearing, and mental health care.
Income support it keeps in place:
- [Statement] Its new leader campaigned for the leadership on widening unemployment benefits within the existing income support, in which mandatory pension schemes replace about 45 percent of an average worker's net earnings, unemployment benefits replace about 48 percent of an average earner's net income in the second month out of work, and a single adult with no other income is guaranteed about 18 percent of the median income, housing support included: raising those benefits to 75 percent of previous earnings, paying them for up to 104 weeks, and letting more workers qualify.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Five of its seven legislators voted against, and two abstained on, the annual budget, which continued the public dental plan, public drug coverage, and subsidized child care without adding new funding for them.
- [Influence] Its leaders twice publicly demanded that the government sign public drug coverage agreements with all remaining regional governments, release the funding promised for them, and finish the first phase of that program.
- [Influence] Its new leader called for funding to extend public drug coverage nationwide after the government's spring economic update added none.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Five of its seven legislators voted against, and two abstained on, the same annual budget, which also made a national school meals program permanent, reaching up to 400,000 more children a year, about 1 percent of residents.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Its legislators introduced two bills to bring mental health and addiction services under the universal public insurance that covers doctor and hospital care.

**Record B**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Votes to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage.
Income support it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Votes to keep in place the existing income support, in which mandatory pension schemes replace about 51 percent of an average worker's net earnings, unemployment benefits replace about 35 percent of an average earner's net income in the second month out of work, and a single adult with no other income is guaranteed about 6 percent of the median income, housing support included.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Voted against a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
- [Influence] At a rally in a district held by the majority party, criticized the local legislator for voting for a budget plan that an official analysis found would require reductions in public health coverage for low-income residents.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Voted against the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Cosponsored a bill to enroll every resident in a single public health plan covering medical, dental, vision, hearing, mental health, and long-term care, with no premiums, deductibles, or copayments.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A upholds universal public insurance and a more generous income floor, and seeks to extend coverage to drugs, dental, and mental health and to raise unemployment benefits to 75 percent; Record B, despite cosponsoring single-payer and opposing cuts, keeps in place a non-universal system with uninsured residents and a much lower income guarantee.

</details>

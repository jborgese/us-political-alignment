# Welfare and health: changes-only pilot, arm B (position wording): blinded spot check

Question: Which record's position, as its evidence shows it, is closer to government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for extending subsidies for private insurance premiums for middle-income households.
- [Official act] Voted against a bill extending public health coverage to all residents.
- [Official act] Voted against a bill narrowing eligibility for existing public health coverage.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for stricter job-search conditions on unemployment benefits, with benefit levels unchanged.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

**Record B**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- No recorded act.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Its three voting legislators voted against a law that raises the standard rate of the main benefit for working-age people above inflation for four years, which the government estimated would raise income for about one in seven households, and that cuts the benefit's health-related supplement by about half for new applicants, leaving current recipients and people with the most severe lifelong conditions unaffected.
- [Official act] Five of its eight legislators voted against, and two voted for, a law removing the rule that limited child-related benefit payments to a family's first two children. The government estimated that the change reaches about one in ten children and lifts about 3 percent of all children out of poverty.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Its deputy leader introduced a bill to limit the main working-age benefit to citizens. About 15 percent of recipients are not citizens, by official figures.
- [Influence] Announced a plan to abolish the main disability benefit and the health-related supplement to the main benefit for working-age adults, replacing them with a regularly reviewed allowance for the most severely ill and locally run support accounts, in order to reduce benefit spending. By its own figures, about 57 percent of the people now receiving those payments would have them changed or withdrawn.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A shows mixed but moderate positions: it backed premium subsidies, blocked a cut to public coverage, opposed universal coverage, and tightened unemployment conditions without cutting benefit levels; Record B mostly opposed benefit expansions such as lifting the two-child limit, sought to restrict the main benefit to citizens, and pressed to abolish disability payments for most recipients, which puts it further from guaranteed support.

</details>

## Pair 2

**Record A**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously against a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Its members voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] None of its members voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously against the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] A former chair of the group introduced, with more than 100 cosponsors, a bill to enroll every resident in a single public health plan with no premiums, deductibles, or copayments.
- [Influence] Held a public hearing to build support for the single public health plan bill, where its chair called passing that bill one of the group's top priorities.

**Record B**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Statement] Said they oppose replacing current coverage with a single public health plan for every resident, which they said would bankrupt rural hospitals.
- [Statement] Said they would pursue other reforms to lower insurance costs rather than extend the expanded premium subsidies, which they said send taxpayers' money to insurers.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Statement] Said protecting the safety-net programs, including public pensions and health coverage for older residents, is essential, and pledged to keep fighting fraud and abuse in them.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A opposed cuts to public health coverage and food assistance, backed extending premium subsidies, and seeks a universal single public health plan, whereas Record B opposes a single public plan and the subsidy extension and only pledges to protect existing programs.

# Economics: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for the government reducing income inequality through taxes and setting firm rules for markets and work?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Taxes it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Its members vote to keep in place the existing tax system, in which tax revenue equals about 26 percent of annual economic output, the top tax rate on earnings is about 44 percent in a typical city, counting regional and local taxes that vary by place, starting at about 9 times the average wage, and the tax rate on company profits is about 26 percent counting regional taxes.
Market and work rules it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Its members vote to keep in place the existing rules, in which the national minimum wage is about 24 percent of the median full-time wage, with higher minimums set by about 60 percent of regional governments, collective agreements cover about 11 percent of employees, and an employer may dismiss most employees at any time without a stated reason, notice, or severance.
Changes to taxes in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously against a budget law that cut national tax revenue by about 1.25 percent of annual economic output a year over ten years, mainly by making permanent lower tax rates on earnings and business income that were due to expire, raising the amount of inherited wealth free of tax, and adding deductions for tips and overtime pay through 2028.
- [Influence] Pressed the majority's leaders in a letter not to extend expiring tax cuts across the board, but to let those for the wealthiest expire and offset the rest with spending cuts and new revenue.
Changes to market and work rules in the period:
- [Official act] Its members who voted all voted against a repeal of a rule that would have capped the fees large banks and credit unions charge on overdrawn accounts at a low fixed amount or at their cost. By the regulator's figures, about one in six households pay such fees.
- [Official act] Its members who voted all voted for a bill restoring the right to bargain collectively to national government employees at agencies where orders of the head of government had removed it. By government data, the share of national government employees represented by a union fell from about 56 to about 38 percent in the year the orders took effect.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

**Record B**

Taxes it keeps in place:
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, keeps in place the existing tax system, in which tax revenue equals about 38 percent of annual economic output, the top tax rate on earnings is about 47 percent, starting at about 4 times the average wage, and the tax rate on company profits is about 30 percent counting local taxes.
Market and work rules it keeps in place:
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, keeps in place the existing rules, in which the minimum wage is about 50 percent of the median full-time wage, collective agreements cover about 49 percent of employees, and an employer with more than 10 employees needs a recognized reason to dismiss anyone employed over six months, must give notice of 4 weeks to 7 months depending on length of service, and must first hear worker representatives where they exist.
Changes to taxes in the period:
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, enacted a law cutting the tax rate on company profits by 5 points in steps from 2028 to 2032, from about 30 to about 25 percent counting local taxes, and allowing faster deduction of investment costs until 2027. The official estimate puts its cost at up to about 0.25 percent of annual economic output a year.
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, enacted a tax law that permanently cut the sales tax on restaurant meals from 19 to 7 percent and raised the deduction for commuting costs. The official estimate puts its cost at about 0.1 percent of annual economic output a year.
Changes to market and work rules in the period:

Further changes it seeks:

- [Statement] Its coalition agreement pledges to replace the daily limit on working hours with a weekly one and to exempt extra pay for overtime from income tax.

# Economics: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for the government reducing income inequality through taxes and setting firm rules for markets and work?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to taxes in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a budget law that cut national tax revenue by about 1.25 percent of annual economic output a year over ten years, mainly by making permanent lower tax rates on earnings and business income that were due to expire, raising the amount of inherited wealth free of tax, and adding deductions for tips and overtime pay through 2028.
Changes to market and work rules in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a repeal of a rule that would have capped the fees large banks and credit unions charge on overdrawn accounts at a low fixed amount or at their cost. By the regulator's figures, about one in six households pay such fees.
- [Official act] Voted against a bill restoring the right to bargain collectively to national government employees at agencies where orders of the head of government had removed it. By government data, the share of national government employees represented by a union fell from about 56 to about 38 percent in the year the orders took effect.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Cosponsored a bill letting workers everywhere in the country decline to join or pay a union as a condition of employment.

**Record B**

Changes to taxes in the period:
- [Official act] Nearly all of its members voted for a budget law that cut national tax revenue by about 1.25 percent of annual economic output a year over ten years, mainly by making permanent lower tax rates on earnings and business income that were due to expire, raising the amount of inherited wealth free of tax, and adding deductions for tips and overtime pay through 2028.
Changes to market and work rules in the period:
- [Official act] Nearly all of its members voted for a repeal of a rule that would have capped the fees large banks and credit unions charge on overdrawn accounts at a low fixed amount or at their cost. By the regulator's figures, about one in six households pay such fees.
- [Official act] Most of its members, including its chair, voted against a bill restoring the right to bargain collectively to national government employees at agencies where orders of the head of government had removed it. By government data, the share of national government employees represented by a union fell from about 56 to about 38 percent in the year the orders took effect.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Released a budget plan that calls for ending the tax on inherited wealth and protecting workers' right not to join or pay a union.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Both records back the same tax cut and the same deregulation, but B's opposition is slightly weaker: only 'most' of its members voted against restoring federal workers' bargaining rights, and its further anti-union and estate-tax aims are influence-tier, whereas A cosponsored a nationwide right-to-work bill as an official act.

</details>

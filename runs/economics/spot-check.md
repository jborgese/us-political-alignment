# Economics: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for the government reducing income inequality through taxes and setting firm rules for markets and work?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to taxes in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously for the final version of a budget law that cut national tax revenue by about 1.25 percent of annual economic output a year over ten years, mainly by making permanent lower tax rates on earnings and business income that were due to expire, raising the amount of inherited wealth free of tax, and adding deductions for tips and overtime pay through 2028.
Changes to market and work rules in the period:
- [Official act] Its members all voted for a repeal of a rule that would have capped the fees large banks and credit unions charge on overdrawn accounts at a low fixed amount or at their cost. By the regulator's figures, about one in six households pay such fees.
- [Official act] Its chair voted against, and none of its members is recorded among the legislators from its party who voted for, a bill restoring the right to bargain collectively to national government employees at agencies where orders of the head of government had removed it. By government data, the share of national government employees represented by a union fell from about 56 to about 38 percent in the year the orders took effect.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Its chair and several of its members cosponsored a bill to repeal the national income, payroll, and inheritance taxes and replace them with a national sales tax.
- [Official act] Several of its members, including a former chair, cosponsored a bill letting workers everywhere in the country decline to join or pay a union as a condition of employment.

**Record B**

Changes to taxes in the period:
- [Official act] In government, enacted, with all but two of its legislators in the lower chamber and all but three in the upper chamber voting for, a budget law that cut national tax revenue by about 1.25 percent of annual economic output a year over ten years, mainly by making permanent lower tax rates on earnings and business income that were due to expire, raising the amount of inherited wealth free of tax, and adding deductions for tips and overtime pay through 2028.
Changes to market and work rules in the period:
- [Official act] In government, enacted, with all but one of its legislators in the lower chamber who voted voting for, a repeal of a rule that would have capped the fees large banks and credit unions charge on overdrawn accounts at a low fixed amount or at their cost. By the regulator's figures, about one in six households pay such fees.
- [Official act] 20 of its legislators voted for, and 195 against, a bill restoring the right to bargain collectively to national government employees at agencies where orders of the head of government had removed it. By government data, the share of national government employees represented by a union fell from about 56 to about 38 percent in the year the orders took effect.
- [Official act] In government, its head of government ordered an end to collective bargaining at about 40 national agencies and agency units.
- [Official act] In government, its head of government ordered national agencies to repeal at least ten existing rules for each new rule they issue.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Statement] Its national platform, adopted before this period, pledges to make the expiring tax cuts permanent, end taxes on tips, and cut regulations. In this period it enacted versions of the first two in the budget law, the second as a temporary deduction.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Both records strongly oppose the question's goals, but B shows some internal dissent (20 legislators voting to restore collective bargaining, a few against the tax cut), while A was unanimous on every vote and further sought to abolish the progressive income and inheritance taxes and impose nationwide right-to-work, so B is marginally less opposed despite its executive orders ending bargaining and cutting rules.

</details>

# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record's position, as its evidence shows it, is closer to government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] All of its legislators in both chambers voted against a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] All of its legislators voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] All of its legislators who voted voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
- [Influence] Its legislators in the upper chamber withheld the votes needed to fund the government for 43 days to demand an extension of the expanded premium subsidies, ended the standoff with only a promise of a vote, and then all voted for a three-year extension that fell short of the votes it needed.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] All of its legislators in both chambers voted against the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Statement] Its national platform, adopted before this period, pledges to make the expanded premium subsidies permanent and to extend public health coverage to the 2.8 million uninsured low-income adults left out of it, under 1 percent of residents. Its votes and its funding standoff in the period, listed above, affirm the first pledge.
- [Statement] The same platform rejects any cut to the public pension and pledges to raise its benefits by raising taxes on the highest earners.

**Record B**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Voted against a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Voted against the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Introduced, as lead sponsor, a bill to enroll every resident in a single public health plan covering medical, dental, vision, hearing, mental health, and long-term care, with no premiums, deductibles, or copayments.
- [Influence] Co-led a public hearing to press colleagues to join the single public health plan bill, arguing it is the only option that guarantees care for everyone.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Both records cast identical votes against the coverage and food-assistance cuts and for the subsidy extension. B additionally sponsors and presses for a universal single public health plan with no cost sharing, a far larger change toward guaranteed coverage than A's pledges to make the subsidies permanent, close a coverage gap affecting under 1 percent of residents, and raise pension benefits.

</details>

## Pair 2

**Record A**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] In government, enacted, with all but two of its legislators in the lower chamber and all but three in the upper chamber voting for, a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] In government, finalized a rule shortening enrollment periods and tightening eligibility checks for subsidized individually purchased health insurance. The official estimate was that it could cost about 0.2 to 0.6 percent of residents their coverage.
- [Official act] 17 of its legislators voted for, and 196 against, a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] 216 of its legislators voted for, and 1 against, a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] In government, enacted, with all but two of its legislators in the lower chamber and all but three in the upper chamber voting for, the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
- [Statement] Its national platform, adopted before this period, pledges no cuts to the public pension or to public health coverage for older residents, and no change to the retirement age. The budget law it enacted in the period cut none of the benefits of that coverage for citizens.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Its head of government asked legislators to pay health subsidy money directly to people, possibly into personal health savings accounts, instead of to insurers, and to require hospitals to post all their prices.

**Record B**

Changes to health coverage in the period:

The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Its members voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] None of its members voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously against the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Endorsed a bill to make the expanded premium subsidies permanent and to reverse the health coverage reductions in the budget law.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

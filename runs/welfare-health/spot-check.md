# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record's position, as its evidence shows it, is closer to government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] In government, passed the annual budget, which continued the public dental plan, public drug coverage, and subsidized child care without adding new funding for them.
- [Official act] In government, extended a public dental plan to all uninsured adults aged 18 to 64 with household income below a set threshold.
- [Official act] In government, provided no new funding for further public drug coverage agreements with regional governments beyond the four already signed.
- [Statement] Said in its budget that it was protecting social programs, naming child care, dental care, and drug coverage.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] In government, passed the same annual budget, which also made a national school meals program permanent, reaching up to 400,000 more children a year, about 1 percent of residents.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

**Record B**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Abstained on the first passage, then voted for the final version, of a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Voted against a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] Voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
- [Influence] As head of a group of legislators, warned party leaders that the group's members would withhold their votes from any funding bill carrying an extension of the expanded premium subsidies.
- [Statement] After the budget law passed, said its work requirements and its limits on the financing arrangements regional governments use would discourage further expansion of public health coverage for low-income residents.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Abstained on the first passage, then voted for the final version, of the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Cosponsored a bill to phase down over eight years the higher national funding share for adults added to low-income public health coverage until it equals the regular share.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A expanded public dental coverage and made school meals permanent, while Record B voted for cuts to low-income health coverage and food assistance, opposed extending premium subsidies, and sought further reductions in public coverage funding.

</details>

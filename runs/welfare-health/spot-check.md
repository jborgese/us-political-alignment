# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Official act] In government, keeps in place a tax-funded health service that is available to every resident and free of charge for doctor and hospital care.
Income support it keeps in place:
- [Official act] In government, keeps in place the existing income support, in which mandatory pension schemes replace about 54 percent of an average worker's net earnings and a single adult with no other income is guaranteed about 54 percent of the median income.
- [Official act] In government, has kept the rule that raises the public pension every year by the highest of prices, wages, or 2.5 percent.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] In government, set day-to-day funding for the public health service to grow by an average of 3 percent a year above inflation in its multi-year spending plan.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] In government, enacted a law that raises the standard rate of the main benefit for working-age people above inflation for four years, which the government estimated would raise income for about one in seven households, and that cuts the benefit's health-related supplement by about half for new applicants, leaving current recipients and people with the most severe lifelong conditions unaffected.
- [Official act] In government, enacted a law removing the rule that limited child-related benefit payments to a family's first two children. The government estimated that the change reaches about one in ten children and lifts about 3 percent of all children out of poverty.
- [Official act] In government, proposed tightening eligibility for the main disability benefit, then withdrew the change after opposition and began a review of how that benefit is assessed.
- [Official act] In government, restored an annual heating payment to about three quarters of retirees, after limiting it the previous winter to retirees receiving low-income benefits.
- [Official act] In government, extended free school meals to all children in households receiving the main benefit for working-age people.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Statement] The party leader, as head of government, announced the intention to create a public care service offering free personal care for older people in the next legislative term, funded in part by making the rule for annual public pension increases less generous from 2030.
- [Statement] The head of government pledged to reduce spending on working-age benefits while ruling out abrupt cuts.

**Record B**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Votes to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage.
Income support it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Votes to keep in place the existing income support, in which mandatory pension schemes replace about 51 percent of an average worker's net earnings and a single adult with no other income is guaranteed about 6 percent of the median income.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Voted against a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Voted for a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] Voted against a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
- [Statement] Pledged to keep working on a cross-party extension of the expanded premium subsidies through the following year.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Voted against the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A keeps in place a universal, free, tax-funded health service and much stronger income guarantees (54% of median versus 6%), and it enacted expansions such as abolishing the two-child benefit limit and raising benefits above inflation. Record B defends a non-universal system against cuts, which is real support but far narrower.

</details>

Income support it keeps in place:

- [Official act] Nearly all of its members voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.

B. Both records backed the same coverage and food-aid cuts. Record A's opposition is more complete and higher-tier: unanimous votes, pressing for faster cuts, no member supporting the subsidy extension, threats to block it, and a cosponsored bill to phase down expansion funding. Record B's votes were only 'nearly all' or 'most', implying some members supported the extension, and its further cuts are only at the influence tier.

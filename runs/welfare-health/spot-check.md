# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Its members vote to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage, less the reductions listed under changes.
Income support it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Its members vote to keep in place the existing income support, in which mandatory pension schemes replace about 51 percent of an average worker's net earnings, unemployment benefits replace about 35 percent of an average earner's net income in the second month out of work, and a single adult with no other income is guaranteed about 6 percent of the median income, housing support included, less the reductions listed under changes.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously for the final version of a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Four of its members voted in committee against advancing an earlier version of that budget law while pressing for its work requirements and spending reductions to take effect sooner, then abstained to let it advance.
- [Official act] Its chair voted against, and none of its members is recorded among the legislators from its party who voted for, a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] Its chair voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
- [Influence] Its chair warned party leaders that members would withhold their votes from any funding bill carrying an extension of the expanded premium subsidies, and several members publicly vowed to block one.
- [Influence] Its governing board declared it would reject any attempt by the other chamber to weaken the spending reductions in the budget bill.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously for the final version of the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Its chair and several of its members cosponsored a bill to phase down over eight years the higher national funding share for adults added to low-income public health coverage until it equals the regular share.

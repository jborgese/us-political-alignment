# Welfare and health: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Health coverage it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Nearly all of its members vote to keep in place the existing system, in which public health coverage is guaranteed to older, disabled, and low-income residents, most others rely on employer or subsidized private insurance, and about 8 percent of residents have no coverage, less the reductions listed under changes.
Income support it keeps in place:
- [Official act] Nearly all of its members vote to keep in place the existing income support, in which mandatory pension schemes replace about 51 percent of an average worker's net earnings, unemployment benefits replace about 35 percent of an average earner's net income in the second month out of work, and a single adult with no other income is guaranteed about 6 percent of the median income, housing support included, less the reductions listed under changes.
Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Nearly all of its members voted for a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Most of its members, including its chair and vice chair, voted against a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] Nearly all of its members voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Nearly all of its members voted for the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Published a framework urging its party to pass a bill that redirects premium subsidies from insurers into personal health accounts held by patients.
- [Influence] In the same framework, urged making all non-citizens ineligible for public health coverage for low-income residents, food assistance, and housing assistance.
- [Influence] In the same framework, urged ending a rule that grants automatic benefit eligibility to people enrolled in other aid programs, and adding asset tests.
- [Influence] In the same framework, urged funding payments that lower out-of-pocket costs for lower-income people with individually purchased insurance, which it said would cut premiums by 11 percent and reduce public spending.
- [Influence] In the same framework, urged requiring public health coverage for low-income residents to pay hospitals the same rate wherever care is delivered, which it counted as a spending reduction.
- [Influence] In the same framework, urged eliminating a dedicated fund for disease prevention and public health programs.

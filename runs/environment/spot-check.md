# Environment: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for cutting greenhouse gas emissions and protecting nature, even at some cost to growth or energy prices?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to emissions limits, carbon pricing, and public spending on cutting emissions in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a resolution, which became law, revoking one regional government's authority to require that all new cars sold there be zero-emission by 2035, a rule several other regional governments had adopted, which that regional government estimated would cut carbon dioxide from cars there by about 395 million tons from 2026 to 2040, about 6 percent of one year's national emissions.
- [Official act] Voted for a resolution, which became law, ending a fee on methane emissions above set limits from oil and gas facilities, whose repeal the official estimate put at about 0.002 percent of a year's economic output a year in lost revenue.
Changes to support for or limits on fossil, nuclear, and renewable energy in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a budget law ending tax credits for new wind and solar power plants that start construction after mid-2026 or start operating after 2027 and for buying electric vehicles, canceling unspent public funds for clean energy, and requiring two offshore oil and gas lease sales a year through 2040, with the tax changes worth about 0.17 percent of a year's economic output a year over ten years by the official estimate.
Changes to pollution limits, nature protection, and environmental review in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a bill, passed by the lower chamber but not yet law, limiting national environmental reviews of projects to effects they closely cause, adding deadlines for agency decisions, and cutting the time to challenge a review in court from six years to 150 days, with no official estimate of how many reviews it covers.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Signed a letter with 37 other legislators of its party urging the tax-writing committee to repeal in full, rather than phase out, the tax credits for clean energy and electric vehicles.
- [Influence] Pressed its party's leaders to end the tax credits for clean energy faster than the draft budget law did, saying it had not meant to leave 40 to 45 percent of them in place.

**Record B**

Changes to emissions limits, carbon pricing, and public spending on cutting emissions in the period:
- [Official act] About four in five of its legislators in the lower chamber who voted voted against a resolution, which became law, revoking one regional government's authority to require that all new cars sold there be zero-emission by 2035, a rule several other regional governments had adopted, which that regional government estimated would cut carbon dioxide from cars there by about 395 million tons from 2026 to 2040, about 6 percent of one year's national emissions.
- [Official act] Six of its legislators in the lower chamber voted for, one voted present, and the rest who voted voted against a resolution, which became law, ending a fee on methane emissions above set limits from oil and gas facilities, whose repeal the official estimate put at about 0.002 percent of a year's economic output a year in lost revenue.
Changes to support for or limits on fossil, nuclear, and renewable energy in the period:
- [Official act] All of its legislators in both chambers voted against a budget law ending tax credits for new wind and solar power plants that start construction after mid-2026 or start operating after 2027 and for buying electric vehicles, canceling unspent public funds for clean energy, and requiring two offshore oil and gas lease sales a year through 2040, with the tax changes worth about 0.17 percent of a year's economic output a year over ten years by the official estimate.
Changes to pollution limits, nature protection, and environmental review in the period:
- [Official act] All but 11 of its legislators in the lower chamber who voted voted against a bill, passed by the lower chamber but not yet law, limiting national environmental reviews of projects to effects they closely cause, adding deadlines for agency decisions, and cutting the time to challenge a review in court from six years to 150 days, with no official estimate of how many reviews it covers.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] About three in four of its legislators in the lower chamber cosponsored a bill restoring the tax credits for wind, solar, and home energy upgrades that the budget law ended, blocking the cancellation of clean energy grants, and speeding approval of renewable energy projects.
- [Statement] Its national platform, adopted before this period, pledges to keep building on the clean energy tax credits, triple clean energy generation by 2030, end tens of billions in oil and gas subsidies, and bring transport emissions to net zero by 2050. Its votes in this period affirm this.

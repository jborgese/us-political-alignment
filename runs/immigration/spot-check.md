# Immigration: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for admitting more immigrants and refugees and for widening the rights of migrants already in the country?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to who may come and stay in the period:
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, had police at the land borders turn back people asking for asylum there. In the latest six months reported they turned back 514, about half of those who asked.
Changes to enforcement, detention, and removal in the period:
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, enacted a law letting the government name safe countries of origin by regulation, without a vote in either chamber, and ending the right to an appointed lawyer for people held in detention before removal.
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, enacted a law adding fast asylum procedures at airports and seaports for applicants from countries with low recognition rates, with removal directly from there if refused, and centers where people awaiting transfer to another country responsible for their claims must stay. The law also lets asylum seekers in reception centers work after three months instead of six.
Changes to migrants' rights and the path to citizenship in the period:
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, enacted a law suspending for two years, except in cases of hardship, family reunification for people with subsidiary protection, which had allowed about 12,000 visas a year, about 0.01 percent of the population.
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, enacted a law ending citizenship after three years of residence for especially well-integrated residents, a route opened the year before, so that five years again became the shortest wait.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] In a governing coalition, approved in cabinet a bill, still before the legislature, moving refugees from one country at war who arrive after a set date from the general benefit for job seekers to the lower benefit for asylum seekers. The bill assumes about 12,000 new arrivals a month, about 0.17 percent of the population a year.

**Record B**

Changes to who may come and stay in the period:
- [Official act] In government, adopted a three-year immigration plan that sets new temporary residents for the coming year at about 0.9 percent of the population, 43 percent fewer than the previous year's target, and holds new permanent residents at about 0.9 percent of the population a year, about 4 percent fewer than the previous year's target.
- [Official act] In government, enacted a law barring referral to a hearing of asylum claims made more than a year after the person first entered, or more than 14 days after crossing the land border between official crossings, and letting the government cancel, suspend or stop processing groups of immigration documents and applications.
- [Official act] In government, adopted a one-time measure in the next year's immigration plan giving permanent residence to up to 200,000 temporary residents already in the country, about 0.5 percent of the population, while leaving yearly targets unchanged.
Changes to enforcement, detention, and removal in the period:
- No recorded act.
Changes to migrants' rights and the path to citizenship in the period:
- [Official act] In government, enacted, with all of its legislators who voted voting for, a law extending citizenship by descent to children born abroad beyond the first generation when the citizen parent had spent at least three years in total in the country before the birth, and restoring it to people excluded by the earlier one-generation limit. Official estimates of the people affected ranged from tens of thousands to about 115,000, up to about 0.3 percent of the population.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Record B pairs restrictive moves (lower temporary resident targets, asylum filing bars) with substantial expansions: permanent residence for up to 200,000 temporary residents and wider citizenship by descent, whereas Record A's acts are almost entirely restrictive (border turnbacks, suspended family reunification, longer path to citizenship, lower benefits), with only a small easing of work access.

</details>

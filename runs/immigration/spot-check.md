# Immigration: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for admitting more immigrants and refugees and for widening the rights of migrants already in the country?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to who may come and stay in the period:
- [Official act] In government, adopted a three-year immigration plan that sets new temporary residents for the coming year at about 0.9 percent of the population, 43 percent fewer than the previous year's target, and holds new permanent residents at about 0.9 percent of the population a year, about 4 percent fewer than the previous year's target.
- [Official act] In government, enacted a law barring referral to a hearing of asylum claims made more than a year after the person first entered, or more than 14 days after crossing the land border between official crossings, and letting the government cancel, suspend or stop processing groups of immigration documents and applications.
- [Official act] In government, adopted a one-time measure in the next year's immigration plan giving permanent residence to up to 200,000 temporary residents already in the country, about 0.5 percent of the population, while leaving yearly targets unchanged.
Changes to enforcement, detention, and removal in the period:
- No recorded act.
Changes to migrants' rights and the path to citizenship in the period:
- [Official act] In government, enacted, with all of its legislators who voted voting for, a law extending citizenship by descent to children born abroad beyond the first generation when the citizen parent had spent at least three years in total in the country before the birth, and restoring it to people excluded by the earlier one-generation limit. Official estimates of the people affected ranged from tens of thousands to about 115,000, up to about 0.3 percent of the population.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

**Record B**

Changes to who may come and stay in the period:
- [Official act] Voted against a bill requiring the government to extend for three years the temporary protection from removal, with work permits, of about 350,000 people from one country, about 0.1 percent of the population, after the government had moved to end it.
Changes to enforcement, detention, and removal in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a budget law adding money for immigration enforcement and border barriers equal to about 0.6 percent of a year's economic output, to be spent mostly over four years, which raises yearly funding for immigration detention about fourfold and adds new fees on asylum applications, asylum seekers' work permits, and temporary protection from removal.
- [Official act] Voted for a law requiring immigration authorities to detain residents without legal status who are charged with, though not convicted of, theft, burglary, shoplifting, or assaulting a police officer. Immigration authorities estimated its first-year cost at about 0.1 percent of annual economic output.
- [Official act] Voted for a budget law adding money for immigration enforcement equal to about 0.23 percent of a year's economic output, to be spent over about three years, mostly on hiring enforcement officers and on enforcement operations.
Changes to migrants' rights and the path to citizenship in the period:
- No recorded act.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Influence] Chaired a group of legislators whose board wrote to the head of government urging that immigration enforcement continue in a city where it had met protests, using every tool including emergency powers to deploy troops, that local governments limiting cooperation with it lose national funding, and that the department in charge of immigration enforcement be fully funded.
- [Statement] Pressed party leaders for a floor vote on a bill writing the head of government's border measures into law, including finishing the border wall, ending the release of migrants awaiting hearings, and tightening asylum standards.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Record A has restrictive cuts and asylum limits, but it also enacted pro-migrant measures: permanent residence for up to 200,000 temporary residents and wider citizenship by descent, whereas Record B consistently voted and pushed for more enforcement, more detention, and an end to temporary protection.

**Record B**

Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

A. Record A votes to extend temporary protection, opposes large enforcement and detention expansions, and cosponsors a broad path to citizenship, while Record B's official acts cut work visas, narrow family-life and refugee rights, and lengthen waits for settlement.

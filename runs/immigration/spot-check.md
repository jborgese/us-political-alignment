# Immigration: blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for admitting more immigrants and refugees and for widening the rights of migrants already in the country?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to who may come and stay in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously for a bill requiring the government to extend for three years the temporary protection from removal, with work permits, of about 350,000 people from one country, about 0.1 percent of the population, after the government had moved to end it.
Changes to enforcement, detention, and removal in the period:
- [Official act] Its members voted unanimously against a budget law adding money for immigration enforcement and border barriers equal to about 0.6 percent of a year's economic output, to be spent mostly over four years, which raises yearly funding for immigration detention about fourfold and adds new fees on asylum applications, asylum seekers' work permits, and temporary protection from removal.
- [Official act] About a third of its members who voted voted for, and the rest voted against, a law requiring immigration authorities to detain residents without legal status who are charged with, though not convicted of, theft, burglary, shoplifting, or assaulting a police officer. Immigration authorities estimated its first-year cost at about 0.1 percent of annual economic output.
- [Official act] All of its members who voted voted against a budget law adding money for immigration enforcement equal to about 0.23 percent of a year's economic output, to be spent over about three years, mostly on hiring enforcement officers and on enforcement operations.
Changes to migrants' rights and the path to citizenship in the period:
- No recorded act.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Nearly nine in ten of its members cosponsored from its introduction a bill giving people brought to the country as children, and holders of temporary protection from removal, permanent residence and a path to citizenship. An independent estimate of an identical earlier version put those potentially eligible at up to about 4.4 million people, about 1.3 percent of the population.
- [Statement] Its working group on immigration proposed a plan that would fund at least 22,000 border agents and more border technology, add 100,000 permanent residence visas a year for graduates of the country's colleges and lift country caps on work visas, give people brought to the country as children a path to citizenship, and give adults without legal status who have lived in the country five years, have no felony record and work or study a temporary legal status that can lead to permanent residence.

**Record B**

# Welfare and health: changes-only pilot, arm A (current wording): blinded spot check

Question: Which record shows stronger support for government-guaranteed health coverage and income support for the general population?

Decide which record fits the question better before opening the rater's answer.

## Pair 1

**Record A**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a budget that reduced funding for public health coverage by about 30 percent and ended that coverage for low-income adults without children.
- [Official act] Voted against extending subsidies for health insurance premiums.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Voted to reduce food and unemployment assistance by about 30 percent and to limit both to two years.
- [Official act] Voted to raise the pension age by three years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Introduced a bill to replace public health coverage for older residents with a fixed payment toward private insurance.
- [Influence] Publicly urged fellow legislators to turn the remaining public health funding into fixed grants that grow more slowly than costs.

**Record B**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Statement] Said they oppose replacing current coverage with a single public health plan for every resident, which they said would bankrupt rural hospitals.
- [Statement] Said they would pursue other reforms to lower insurance costs rather than extend the expanded premium subsidies, which they said send taxpayers' money to insurers.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Statement] Said protecting the safety-net programs, including public pensions and health coverage for older residents, is essential, and pledged to keep fighting fraud and abuse in them.
Further changes it seeks:
- No recorded act.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

B. Record A has official acts that cut public health coverage, end coverage for childless low-income adults, cut food and unemployment aid, raise the pension age, and seek to privatize coverage for older residents, while Record B's statements oppose some expansions but pledge to protect existing safety-net programs, a weaker anti-support position than A's documented cuts.

</details>

## Pair 2

**Record A**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Statement] Before the budget bill's final passage, said it was not yet good enough, and pressed for work requirements on public health coverage to start sooner and for an end to paying a higher national share for working-age adults than for the most vulnerable.
- [Statement] Wrote that colleagues should let the expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased insurance expire, calling them a costly temporary measure from the pandemic.
Changes to income support in the period:
- No recorded act.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Statement] Said national spending on public health coverage was driving deficits, and called for wider use of health savings accounts and more competition in health care instead.

**Record B**

Changes to health coverage in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for a budget law that reduced national funding for public health coverage for low-income residents by about 14 percent over ten years and added work requirements to that coverage. The official estimate is that about 3 percent of residents lose health coverage as a result.
- [Official act] Voted against a three-year extension of expanded premium subsidies for individually purchased health insurance. About 7 percent of residents hold that insurance, and an independent estimate was that subsidized buyers' average premium payment would more than double without the extension.
- [Official act] Voted for a bill that loosened rules for employer and association health plans and funded payments that reduce cost sharing in individual insurance. The official estimate was that it would lower pre-subsidy premiums for individual insurance by about 11 percent and reduce the number of residents with coverage by about 0.03 percent.
- [Official act] In committee, voted against advancing an earlier version of that budget law while pressing for its work requirements and spending reductions to take effect sooner, then abstained to let it advance once the work requirements were moved earlier.
- [Influence] Publicly urged party colleagues to let the expanded premium subsidies expire, and said they should not expect support for any deal that extends them.
- [Statement] After the committee vote, said the budget bill needed further changes before final passage, including an end to the financing arrangements that let regional governments draw additional national funds for public health coverage.
Changes to income support in the period:
- [Official act] Voted for the same budget law, which also reduced national funding for food assistance for low-income households by about 16 percent over ten years.
Further changes it seeks:
- [Official act] Introduced a bill, and filed an amendment to the budget bill, to phase down over eight years the higher national funding share for adults added to low-income public health coverage until it equals the regular share, and to let regional governments narrow eligibility for that group.
- [Influence] Led a letter, signed by 19 other legislators, urging colleagues to lower the national funding share for adults added to low-income public health coverage to the regular share, to limit the financing arrangements regional governments use to draw national funds, and to rule out further expansion of that coverage.

<details><summary>Rater's answer</summary>

A. Both records oppose expanding coverage, but B voted for and pressed for larger cuts to public health coverage, premium subsidies, and food assistance, and sought further eligibility narrowing, while A's opposition is limited to statements seeking narrower changes, so A is the less opposed record.

</details>
